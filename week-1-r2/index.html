  2. Where we started. Standard broadcasts going to everyone on the list on the same day. Open rates looked fine. Revenue was flat. The list was being shouted at, not written to.
  3. First move. Split the list into three groups based on behaviour, not size. Group A bought in the last three months. Group B bought six to eighteen months ago and had gone quiet. Group C had never bought but still opened emails.
  4. Group A is the easiest group to underpay attention to, because they already pay you. We sent them an early access email twenty-four hours before anything public. It read as a thank-you, not a promotion. Revenue from this group doubled first.
  5. Group B needed a specific reason to come back. Not "we miss you." A new product that matched something they bought before. One sentence. One link. It brought back a real slice of the group.
  6. Group C needed the cleanest offer. One hook. One clear saving. Four emails through the window with rising urgency. Accept that some of this group never buys and write for the ones who will.
  7. Send times mattered more than subject lines. Each group got a different send time that matched when their past opens had landed. No clever trick. Just reading what the data already said.
  8. Result. Revenue per lead almost doubled over the window [verify with Muddassir: exact figure]. Most of the lift came from Group A, at the smallest send volume. The list we had was bigger than we were treating it.
